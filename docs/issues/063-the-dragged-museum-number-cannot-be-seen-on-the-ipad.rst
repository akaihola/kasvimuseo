Problem
=======

As reported from the iPad (7th generation, iPadOS 18.7.9, Safari) on
2026-08-02, on ``/kasvimuseo/planting-labels/``:

**The museum number is not visible while it is being dragged.**

That is the whole report. It arrived from the same look at the device that
confirmed 056's first half, and it is 045's drag layer rather than 056's text
fitter, which is why it is a file of its own: ``#drag-number`` sits outside
``#labels``, takes none of the rules 056 is about, and is drawn by the copy
045's large half added.

What the report says is **not** known: whether the number still moves between
labels and is merely invisible on the way, or whether the gesture does nothing
at all. Nothing in the two sections below assumes either -- they were written
before there was an answer, and they are left as they were measured. The answer
came from the device a day later, under "What the device answered", and it is
the first one: the sheet rearranges, and nothing is drawn.

Where the number comes from
===========================

``kasvimuseo/templates/kasvimuseo/reports/planting-labels.html`` draws a copy
of the number being dragged under the pointer for the whole gesture -- what the
HTML5 drag layer used to get for free as the browser's drag image, and what
045's "the number itself needed drawing" section is about. The copy is
``#drag-number``, a ``<p class="observation-id">`` outside ``#labels``, so
046's ``zoom: 0.5`` does not apply to it, and ``dragStart`` therefore has to
tell it what size to be::

    rect = el.getBoundingClientRect(),
    drawn = rect.width / el.offsetWidth || 1,
    fontSize = parseFloat(window.getComputedStyle(el).fontSize) * drawn;

``rect`` is in drawn pixels and ``offsetWidth`` in laid-out ones, and those are
the two coordinate systems 746ce71 -- the commit behind 056 -- says Safari
reports differently inside a ``zoom``\ ed subtree. So a ``font-size`` of zero,
or of something absurd, is the first thing the report says to measure. It was
measured, and it is not what happens.

What was measured
=================

Mid-gesture, with the pointer still down over the second label, in Playwright
at the device's landscape metrics -- 1080 x 810, ``has_touch``, ``is_mobile``
and the iPad's own user agent, so the template's ``isIOS`` JavaScript branch is
the one running. The emulated viewport is the device's own CSS pixel count, so
these numbers are in the units the tablet uses.

====================== ============= =============== ===============
``#drag-number``       WebKit, mouse Chromium, mouse Chromium, touch
====================== ============= =============== ===============
computed ``font-size`` 16.83px       16.58px         16.60px
box, w x h             28.0 x 26.6   22.1 x 25.9     22.1 x 26.0
holds the pointer      yes           yes             yes
``opacity``            1             1               1
``color``              black         black           black
``background``         white         white           white
``z-index``            10            10              10
``visibility``         ``visible``   ``visible``     ``visible``
``display``            ``block``     ``block``       ``block``
on screen              yes           yes             yes
text                   ``11``        ``11``          ``11``
====================== ============= =============== ===============

And the inputs the size is computed from, identical in both engines::

    getComputedStyle(number).fontSize        32px   (the page's p { 24pt })
    getBoundingClientRect().width            38.65px
    offsetWidth                              76px
    ratio                                    0.50856
    getComputedStyle(#labels).zoom           0.5

The ratio is the ``zoom``, to four decimal places and a rotation: the number
carries ``transform: rotate(-3deg)``, so its client rectangle is the
axis-aligned box of a tilted one and is wider than the element by
``sin(3 deg)`` of its height. That is the whole of the difference between
0.50856 and 0.5.

The gesture around those numbers works, here. 045's emulated touch tests --
a number moved between labels by finger, a tap that moves nothing, a cancelled
gesture, a release off the sheet, the save cycle -- all pass, which is what the
report means by "whatever this is, it is not the gesture". Note what that is
worth and what it is not: it establishes that the drag layer works in Chromium
with touch emulation, and it cannot establish anything about iOS Safari, which
is where the report comes from. The report's open fork -- does the number still
move on the device -- is not closed by a green suite here, and this file does
not treat it as closed. It was closed the next day by somebody looking at the
tablet, which is a different kind of evidence and is recorded separately.

**The report does not reproduce.** In neither engine, by mouse or by finger, on
a scrolled page or an unscrolled one -- with the sheet scrolled 60px the copy
still lands under the pointer in both, which is the "positioned off-screen"
possibility measured rather than argued. The copy is drawn, at half the
number's own font size, opaque, black on white, above the sheet, inside the
viewport, and under the pointer that is dragging it.

What the ratio can and cannot do
================================

Since the ratio is the suspect the report names first, it was driven through
its degenerate values directly -- the same arithmetic, the same inline
declaration, and the browser asked what it made of the result:

========================= ======= ========= ==============================
rect / offsetWidth        ratio   asked for drawn at
========================= ======= ========= ==============================
0 / 76                    1       32px      32px, 43 x 50
38.65 / 0                 ``inf`` ``infpx`` 32px, 43 x 50
0 / 0                     1       32px      32px, 43 x 50
38.65 / 76 (the real one) 0.50855 16.27px   16.27px, 22 x 25
76 / 38.65 (inverted)     1.96636 62.92px   62.92px, 84 x 98
1e-9 / 76                 1.3e-11 4.2e-10px **0px, 0 x 0**
1e9 / 76                  1.3e7   4.2e8px   1e4px, clamped (1e6 in WebKit)
========================= ======= ========= ==============================

Read from that table, and this is the part that decides the issue:

* **A zero ``font-size`` cannot come from a zero rectangle.** ``|| 1`` catches
  it -- ``0/76`` is falsy and becomes 1 -- so the copy is drawn at the number's
  full 32px, twice its size on the sheet rather than none of it. The same
  fallback catches ``0/0``.
* **A zero ``offsetWidth`` cannot make it disappear either.** The ratio is
  ``Infinity``, which makes the inline declaration invalid, which the browser
  drops -- and the page's own unscoped ``p { font-size: 24pt }`` matches
  ``#drag-number`` too, so what is left is again 32px.
* **An inverted ratio is visible.** If Safari reported the rectangle in
  laid-out pixels and ``offsetWidth`` in drawn ones -- the worst inversion a
  ``zoom`` of 0.5 could produce -- the copy is 62.9px, and the whole plausible
  range of ratios a 0.5 zoom can generate, 0.25 through 4, is 8px to 128px.
  Every one of those is a number a person can see; several are hard to miss.
* **The only invisible outcome needs a ratio around 1e-11**, four decimal
  orders below anything a factor of two can produce. The clamped end is not
  invisible either: it is a digit taller than the screen.

The first of those bullets is load-bearing enough that it is now a test rather
than a reading. ``test_a_number_that_measures_no_width_is_still_drawn_when_dragged``
patches ``getBoundingClientRect`` on the museum numbers to report a width of
zero -- and only their width, so hit-testing and the grab offsets are
untouched -- drags one, and asserts the copy is drawn at the fallback size.
Deleting the ``|| 1`` from the template turns it red, and what it goes red
*with* is worth recording, because it is the reported symptom exactly::

    fontSize: 0    box: 0 x 0    opacity: 1    onScreen: True

That is what an unguarded zero measurement looks like from the outside: a copy
that is present, opaque, correctly positioned and impossible to see. So the
mechanism the report suspects is real and would produce precisely what was
reported -- and the guard against it is in the template today, is exercised by
a test as of this issue, and cannot be reached by any measurement a ``zoom`` of
0.5 produces.

One more thing points the same way, from the other direction. 062 measured
that WebKit on this host never draws this page's text below **9 used pixels**;
it found that inside 046's ``zoom``, and it says in as many words that the drag
copy is not what it is about, but the floor is a property of the engine family
rather than of the zoom. So on the engine nearest the device, a computed size
small enough to disappear is a size that engine declines to draw. That is
emulation and not the tablet -- as 062 also says, a minimum font size is an
embedder's setting -- but it is one more reason the small-size branch of this
report is the unlikely one.

So the ratio is ruled out as a cause of *invisibility*. It can make the copy
half size, double size or, at the extreme, absurd -- and none of those is
"cannot be seen".

What the device answered
========================

Everything above this section was measured on this host, and the section below
it was written while the report's open fork was still open. On **2026-08-03**
the maintainer answered the two looking questions from the tablet -- the ones
listed under "What is left" as the pair that decides whether anything is
written at all:

============================================ ==========================
asked                                        answered
============================================ ==========================
Drag a number onto another label of the same **Yes, it rearranges**
species and lift -- does the sheet
rearrange?
Slide the finger a centimetre, stop, and     **Nothing anywhere**
look beside the contact point -- what is
there?
============================================ ==========================

Both are observations by eye, not instrument readings, which is what they are
worth: no number was taken, and none of the measurements in the table above has
a counterpart from the device. What they settle they settle by elimination.

* **The drag layer works on the device.** The gesture does what 045 built it to
  do, so this is a drawing issue and not a reopening of 045. That is the fork
  the report parked, closed from the tablet.
* **The copy is not under the finger.** The candidate this file ranked first --
  a correctly drawn 4 mm number covered by a contact patch twice its width --
  predicts the number showing at the edges of the fingertip, because
  ``grabX`` / ``grabY`` hold the copy at the point of itself that was grabbed
  rather than centring it on the pointer. Looking beside the finger is exactly
  the test of that prediction, and the prediction failed.

Note what the pair does **not** leave standing. The three candidates below were
written as an exhaustive list, and between these two answers and the arithmetic
in "What the ratio can and cannot do", none of them survives as written:
candidate 1 is eliminated by the second answer, candidate 3 by the first, and
candidate 2 predicts a copy at *half* size -- 12pt where 24pt was declared --
which is a smaller number rather than no number. So the honest state of this
issue is not "one of three, narrowed to one" but **something not yet named**:
iOS Safari draws nothing where both engines here draw an opaque 22 x 26 box
under the pointer, while the gesture underneath it works.

That is a sharper question than the one this file opened with, and it is the
kind an instrument answers rather than a further guess. It is why "What is
left" below is now one item and not four, and why the ``Status`` does not move:
nothing here can watch the thing that differs.

What is left to suspect
=======================

Written on 2026-08-02, before the device answered, and left as it was: the
section above records which of these the tablet eliminated and why none of them
now stands as written. In the order the evidence here supported at the time.

**1. The copy is drawn correctly and the finger is on top of it.**
*Eliminated by the device on 2026-08-03 -- there is nothing beside the finger
either.* It was the leading candidate, and it is arithmetic rather than a
hunch. The copy measures
22 x 26 CSS pixels. An iPad (7th generation) is 810 x 1080 CSS pixels on a
10.2-inch, 264 ppi screen at 2x, so one CSS pixel is 0.192 mm and the copy is
about **4.3 x 5.0 mm**. Apple's own smallest recommended touch target is 44 pt,
which is 8.5 mm, and a fingertip's contact patch is larger still. ``dragStart``
records ``grabX`` / ``grabY`` so the copy keeps the spot of itself the pointer
took hold of -- deliberately, so it does not jump -- which puts the pointer
*inside* the copy for the whole gesture; ``test_the_dragged_number_follows_the_finger``
asserts exactly that. Under a mouse cursor that is feedback. Under a fingertip
roughly twice its width, it is underneath the finger. Every measurement in this
file is consistent with this, and no measurement available here can confirm it.

**2. The iOS branch of the stylesheet, which nothing here applies.**
*Not eliminated, but it does not explain the report on its own: what it
predicts is a copy at half size, and half of a readable number is a readable
number.* On the
device -- and only there -- ``@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none)`` is true,
and ``#labels p { font-size: var(--fit-screen-size, 12pt) }`` matches the
museum numbers as well as the classification line it was written for.
``--fit-screen-size`` is set inline by the fitter on the heading and the
classification only; on a museum number it is undefined, measured empty in both
engines, so the numbers take the 12pt fallback. That is half of the declared
24pt, which is what 056's compensation *wants* on a device where ``zoom``
scales boxes and not text -- so it is very likely benign, and it is written
down here because it is the one thing in the size chain that differs between
the device and every browser available here. It halves the copy too, by way of
the computed value ``dragStart`` reads.

**3. Something about the gesture on the device.**
*Eliminated by the device on 2026-08-03 -- the sheet rearranges, so the gesture
arrives.* 045's file already lists what
was never confirmed there: that iOS Safari raises ``pointerdown`` /
``pointermove`` / ``pointerup`` from a finger on this page at all, and whether
Safari's own gestures -- the selection loupe, the tap-and-hold callout, an edge
swipe -- interrupt one. If the answer to the report's open fork is "the number
does not move either", then this is not a drawing issue at all and 045 is
reopened rather than this issue being about a copy.

What emulation cannot see
=========================

The same three limits 056 records, restated for this report, because they are
why the list above cannot be shortened from here:

* **The CSS half of the iOS branch never applies.**
  ``CSS.supports('-webkit-touch-callout', 'none')`` is ``false`` in both
  Playwright engines on this host, measured again for this issue. So candidate
  2 above cannot be watched, only read.
* **The premise cannot be checked.** In both engines ``getComputedStyle``
  inside the ``zoom: 0.5`` subtree reports the declared 32px, and the
  rect-to-``offsetWidth`` ratio is the zoom. The disagreement 746ce71 describes
  is not present here, so the arithmetic that matters runs only where nothing
  here can watch it.
* **WebKit is not iOS Safari.** It is the nearest engine that runs on this
  host, and since 061 the whole suite runs in it, so the measurements above are
  taken there rather than asserted about there. It still cannot be given a
  touch drag -- ``Input.dispatchTouchEvent`` is a Chrome DevTools call and
  ``new_cdp_session`` is Chromium-only, which is why 061 skips touch drags in
  WebKit rather than faking them -- so the WebKit column above is the mouse
  test. Both pointer types go through the same ``dragStart``, which is where
  the size is computed, so what that costs is the touch half of the gesture and
  not the arithmetic.

What is left
============

For whoever has the iPad. The two looking questions were answered on
2026-08-03 -- see "What the device answered" -- and answering them took the
list from four items to one, by ruling out the two candidates a person could
rule out by eye. What is left is the one that needs an instrument:

1. **What does ``#drag-number`` actually measure on the device, mid-drag?**
   Safari's Web Inspector attached to the tablet, the drag held, and the same
   row of properties the table under "What was measured" lists for the two
   engines here: computed ``font-size``, the bounding rectangle, ``opacity``,
   ``color``, ``z-index``, ``visibility``, ``display``, and where the box is
   relative to the viewport and to the pointer. Any one of them differing from
   that table names the cause; all of them matching it moves the question to
   what is painted rather than what is laid out. The element does exist to be
   inspected -- the gesture it belongs to demonstrably runs -- so this is a
   reading rather than a hunt. The iPad Safari debugging setup 056's file
   mentions is what makes it cheap, and this is now the second issue waiting on
   it.

Two things follow that measurement rather than preceding it, and neither should
be written before it:

* **If the copy is drawn somewhere off-screen or at an absurd size**, the cause
  is in the coordinate arithmetic ``dragStart`` performs, and the fix is
  testable here the moment the reading says which term is wrong -- the ratio
  table under "What the ratio can and cannot do" is the shape that check would
  take.
* **If the copy measures exactly what it measures here**, then it is laid out
  correctly and not painted, which is a compositing question about a
  ``position: fixed`` element above a ``zoom``\ ed subtree in iOS Safari, and
  it is the point at which this stops being a template bug at all.

The one candidate this file will *not* return to without a reason is drawing
the copy offset from the contact point, or larger, on a touch pointer. That was
the fix for the finger-on-top-of-it reading, the device eliminated that
reading, and it would change what 045 ruled -- its preview ruling was that a
*label*-sized thing following the finger is worse than none, and
``test_the_dragged_number_follows_the_finger`` pins the current answer.

Nothing on that list is a change to this repository, which is what ``Accepted``
means here.

Traps found on the way
======================

* **The suspect the report leads with is already guarded.** ``|| 1`` in
  ``dragStart`` is what makes ``font-size: 0`` unreachable from a zero
  measurement, and the guard is easy to read past -- ``rect.width /
  el.offsetWidth || 1`` binds as ``(rect.width / el.offsetWidth) || 1``, so it
  catches ``0``, ``NaN`` and every other falsy result. Measuring it was worth
  more than reading it: the table above is what turns "probably fine" into a
  ruled-out cause. It was also unpinned, which is the part that would have
  aged badly -- an issue whose ruling rests on a guard that no test exercises
  is one refactor away from being wrong on the page and right in the file.
* **The label sheet does not scroll at the iPad's landscape viewport**, on the
  browser suite's seeded data: two labels at 046's 50 % zoom fit inside 810px
  with room to spare, so ``window.scrollTo`` moves nothing and a test that
  believes it has scrolled is measuring the unscrolled case. The scrolled
  measurement above was taken at a deliberately short viewport, with the scroll
  position asserted before the drag rather than assumed.
* **Mobile text autosizing confounds a stylesheet experiment.** Applying the
  iOS ``@supports`` rules by hand, with the gate removed, to see what they do
  to the numbers does not measure what it looks like it measures: both engines
  inflate small text in an ``is_mobile`` context, and a 12pt number came back
  at 32px. What candidate 2 above rests on is the cascade -- that
  ``--fit-screen-size`` is undefined on a museum number, which reads out as an
  empty string and is not affected by autosizing -- and not on a font size
  measured through that experiment.
